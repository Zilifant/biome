## ⚠⚠ The four things P10 learned

### 1. ⚠⚠ A derived-on-read projection can be quietly coupled to a mechanism switch

`group.centre` had an obvious implementation — read `world.groupCentres`, which
`GroupSystem` already maintains — and it is **wrong**. That map is rebuilt by
`#rally`, and only when `config.groups.rallyEnabled`, so the block would report
`null` for every group the moment somebody switched the rally off, which is a
**control arm this suite uses**. Measured on the demo at tick 200:
`groupCentres.size` is **28 with the rally on and 0 with it off, against 28 live
records either way**.

> ⚠ **Before reading a transient map, ask which switch populates it.** A cached
> derivation is only as available as the system that caches it, and an observability
> field that vanishes on a control arm is worse than no field: it makes the arm look
> broken.

Both arms are asserted in `protocol-v34.test.js`, because "it works with the rally
on" is exactly what the wrong version also says.

### 2. ⚠ The plan's claim about A43 was wrong, and checking it was the work

BEHAVIOR-PLAN's P10 says P2's two-bands test "is the fragmentation assertion A43
says nobody has written". It is not. That test asserts a **centroid** — one animal's
steering input, on one tick, in a two-band sandbox — where A43 asks for a population
fragmentation **outcome**. What P10 does deliver is the *measure* A43's own
resolution in DOCS §1 was waiting on, which is a different and smaller thing.
A43 is therefore **narrowed and documented**, not closed.

> ⚠ **A plan asserting that an existing test closes an action item is a claim to
> verify, not an instruction to follow.** Read the item before agreeing with the
> plan about it. Three of P10's four doc items were genuinely already done; the
> fourth was the one the plan was confident about.

### 3. ⚠ Two of the three commitments had no inspection at all, and the plan predates them

P10's list names only P8's consensus, because it was written before P7 and P9
existed. So `social.rally` and `social.charge` were added beside it. The general
shape worth carrying: **a mechanism whose whole purpose is to outlive its cue erases
its own evidence.** By the time a pursuit is what is happening, `defendingId` has
already gone null, the threat is out of perception, and `defendUntil` was the only
thing left — so a commitment mechanism is precisely the kind that cannot be observed
through the fields that were already there.

### 4. ⚠ Saturation was made visible as a *sample*, and the alternative was worse

The `groups` store's cap has been silent since phase 3: at the cap `found()` returns
null with no event, metric or log. P10 reports `capacity` and `saturated` in the
aggregate, which is a **state sample at the metrics stagger of 50 ticks** — a store
that fills and empties between two samples is not counted, and the aggregate says so.
Counting *refusals* was considered and rejected: a cumulative counter is history
rather than state, so it would read differently after a restore, and it would need
persisting to fix that — a save-format bump for an observability counter measuring a
failure mode that has never once occurred (peak 37 records against a cap of 192).

## ⚠⚠ The lessons worth carrying past this plan

These are the ones that cost time more than once. Each has a phase behind it.

### 1. ⚠⚠ A guard tested on the wrong species is not tested — **six phases, six times**

`MIXER` (P2), `HOLDER` (P3), `worth *= calfWeight` (P4), P7's unreachable dispersal
gate, P8's double charge, P9's `alarmFlee` collision. Every one of them was a
mechanism that could be **deleted with the suite still green**, because the fixture
species' other numbers made the guard unreachable.

> ⚠ **A weight tested only at 1, at 0, or at a value that saturates its own cap is
> not tested.** Pick a fixture number that is none of those, then delete the multiply
> and watch the test fail. P10 followed this with `leadAgeWeight: 0.5`.

### 2. ⚠⚠ Before setting a weight, list what it is compared against *in the situation it fires in*

P9 shipped `chargeWeight: 0.7` sized against `fleeWeight: 2.0` and was **inert** —
the real competitor is `alarmFlee` (`fleeWeight × 0.75`), which fires exactly when no
threat is perceived, which is exactly when a pursuit exists. The mechanism formed
commitments and never once acted on one; the demo could not have told anyone.

### 3. ⚠⚠ A ttl on an intent commits to nothing — a commitment must live in the utility

`#intentFor` is rebuilt from the winning action **every tick**, and only the `wander`
branch reads a prior intent's ttl as a continuation. Both P8's consensus and P9's
pursuit therefore keep *state that keeps the score non-zero*. First thing to check if
a later phase wants an animal to keep doing something after its reason has gone.

### 4. ⚠⚠ Ask what tick the phenomenon starts at, then measure past it — three instances

A56 was ten times worse than four phases of measurement said, because every demo
assertion stopped at 1500 ticks and the churn arrives with the first wave of deaths
(212 membership events at tick 1000, **3502** at 5000). P8's three seeds at 3000
ticks said the *opposite* of five seeds at 8000, because both arms were still falling
steeply at 3000 — comparing two curves mid-fall compares their phase, not their
level. P9's off arm is byte-identical for 2300–3700 ticks and then is not.
`test/groups.slow.test.js` exists because of this and runs to 5000.

### 5. ⚠ A weight is not the lever; the dead band is

Do not measure a centroid mechanism by where animals end up. `herd` is a **dead-band**
controller — an animal closes up only past `herdDistance`, then stops — so moving the
target point changes headings tick by tick and leaves the equilibrium alone. P4's
three attempts at a spatial consequence all came back inside seed noise. P7 and P8
are on the other side of that line: they bend `wander`, which has no dead band, and
both moved animals on the first measurement. **If a later phase wants a herd to
genuinely reorganize, the number is `behavior.herdDistance`.**

### 6. ⚠ A 0/0 is not a crash here, it is a silent behaviour deletion

`NaN` loses every `argmaxUtility` comparison, so a NaN utility means an action is
**never chosen again** with nothing anywhere throwing. Three of the family: P3's
`pullScale = 0/0`, P7's `clamp01(undefined)` → a permanent `entity.x = NaN`, and
P8's `atan2(0, 0)` — the nastiest, because it does not look like an error, it looks
like a whole wildebeest herd deciding to march east. All three are guarded and the
guards are load-bearing.

### 7. ⚠ The off-arm proof needs *every* switch the phase added, and strip new fields before hashing

P8's control is `consensus.enabled: false` **and** `groups.leadWeight: 0` together;
each alone leaves the other's difference in, and both genuinely move the world.
⚠ Entities serialize whole, so a save from this tree can never equal one from before
it — strip the new entity fields before comparing, because the claim is about the
*world*, not the record format.

```js
createDemoSimulation({ seed, config: { consensus: { enabled: false }, groups: { leadWeight: 0 } } })
```

### 8. ⚠ `updateInterval` can be part of the mechanism, not only the cost

Because a label re-decides only on a tick `HerdConsensusSystem` runs, commitments
quantize onto multiples of `consensus.updateInterval` (5) — which is what makes herds
founded ticks apart still expire **together**, and therefore what turns n independent
re-decisions into one collective one. Setting it to 1 makes the front *blurrier*, not
more exact. The trade: `commitTicks` is a floor rounded up to the cadence, so a
commitment can outlast its ttl by up to four ticks, and `DecisionSystem` gates on the
fields rather than on the clock.

## Landmines

- ⚠⚠ **`git checkout` cannot restore an untracked file.** A mutation-test loop that
  backs up with `cp` into a directory that does not exist will silently stack its
  mutations. Make the directory first, or check `cp`'s exit code.
- ⚠ **`world.entities.all()` returns an iterator, not an array.** `.find()` works
  (iterator helpers), `.filter(…).length` is `undefined` — a silent `false` in an
  assertion rather than an error. Spread it: `[...world.entities.all()]`.
  `world.groups.all()` *does* return an array.
- **Two tests fish for a rare emergent event in a fixed window** and break on
  behaviour changes: [`test/groups.test.js`](../test/groups.test.js) and
  [`test/protocol-v29.test.js`](../test/protocol-v29.test.js) (first-carcass-theft).
  Neither moved at P8, P9 or P10, but the failure mode is *late*, never absent. ⚠ A
  third did fire at P9: `cooperation.test.js`'s batch-2 `soloMobbed` cell, now widened
  to six seeds `[42, 2, 3, 5, 1, 7]` for n=10. An audit found **13 tests of this
  shape**; `carcass.slow` and `mate-choice` are next closest to the edge, and the
  mobbing cell has gone first four times running. **This is why A43's remaining half
  was not closed with a demo assertion.**
- **A test that measures an equilibrium is measuring the seed** unless you have shown
  otherwise. Run it on five seeds before you believe it.
- **Measure cost with entity counts matched**, 450 ticks, five interleaved rounds,
  discard round 1 (JIT). ⚠⚠ **The machine drifted ~15% between P8's cost measurement
  and P9's**, same day, no code in between. Both tables in `BENCHMARK.md` are
  interleaved pairs for exactly that reason; do not read across them.
- ⚠ `perl -0pi -e 's/…/…/'` without `/g` replaces **only the first match**.
- ⚠ The 5 `presets.test.js` HTTP suites are **cancelled** in a sandboxed shell
  (`listen EPERM`) — pre-existing, unrelated, ignore them.

## Test workflow

```bash
npm run test:fast   # ~7 min — use this in the edit loop
npm test            # ~14 min, everything — before committing
npm run test:ui     # Playwright, SEPARATE, not part of npm test — ⚠ hangs in
                    #   teardown in a sandboxed shell; see above
```

`test:fast` skips demo/persistence/determinism suites and the `*.slow.test.js` files.
⚠ `--test-skip-pattern` is **silently ignored if it comes after the file arguments**;
the npm script has the order right, don't reshuffle it.

⚠ `test/cooperation.test.js` got ~50% slower at P9 (its batch-2 block runs six
6000-tick demo worlds). `--test-name-pattern` does *not* help — that block builds its
sample in an IIFE at describe time and runs whatever you select.

New size-independent guards should use
[`test/helpers/smallDemo.js`](../test/helpers/smallDemo.js) (~3.9× faster) — but read its
header: legitimate for determinism and round-trip claims, **never** for ecological
ones.

## Open threads

- ⬜ **A43 is narrowed, not closed** — the *measure* exists (`groups.spread`), a
  population label-split *reading* does not. See `ACTION-ITEMS.md` A43 for why it was
  not written as a demo assertion.
- ✅ **Renderer fixtures are current** as of 2026-08-06, at v34, after all six
  behavioural phases. The next behavioural change to the demo makes them stale again;
  DOCS §12's rule is to regenerate after the last behavioural change, not at a version
  bump.
- ⚠ **v34 is a version the renderer *speaks* but does not *show*.** Making the
  inspector display the new blocks is the renderer's roadmap item
  (`src/renderer/DOCS-RENDERER.md`), not this plan's. The engine side owed the
  constant, the fixtures and a passing UI suite, and nothing beyond that.
- ⚠ **`behavior.leadAgeWeight` is inert in short runs, by construction.** Seniority is
  `senescent` or nothing and buffalo reach `adultUntil: 11000`, so a 1500-tick demo
  contains no matriarch at all. What `groups.leadWeight` moves in a short run is plain
  `dominanceOf` (mass and condition). Measuring the age half needs a horizon past
  11 000 ticks.
- ⚠ **P9 is the mechanism most likely to be judged by the wrong number.** It fires ~25
  ticks in 4000 in the demo (see the prevalence table above: **zero** live pursuits at
  tick 1500 on seed 42), so any population or survival reading of it is noise by
  construction. What it changes is the *shape* of the rare tick. Anyone re-tuning
  `chargeWeight` should re-read the `alarmFlee` collision first.
- ⚠ **A label re-decides with whoever is free that tick**, so a straggler whose
  commitment lapsed out of phase re-decides by itself — a herd is mostly, not
  perfectly, in phase. If a herd ever looks like it is fanning out, `updateInterval`
  is the number to look at, not `commitTicks`.
- ⚠ **A consensus replaces the *whole* migration drift, and that drift multiplexes
  thirst** — so a thirsty animal in a herd that is not thirsty loses its long-range
  water cue for up to 60 ticks. That is why the buffalo declares 0.6 rather than 1.0.
  Measured: wildebeest dehydration deaths **655 with the consensus against 708
  without**, i.e. down. ⚠ Re-check it if anyone raises a `consensusWeight`.
- **The wildebeest associates with the zebra as of P3** and declares no
  `associationPull`, deliberately — the in-roster control arm saying the two fields are
  separable. Do not give it one without measuring.
- ✅ **Nothing reassembles a torn herd label and nothing is scheduled to** — a herd that
  tears in half is meant to become two herds, which now both keep marching (P7 brings a
  scattered *record* back; P8 keeps a scattered *label* pointed the same way).
- ⚠ **The demo's buffalo are collapsing** — 93 alive at t3000, 18 at t9000 on seed 42.
  **Pre-existing and not P5's or P8's**: an identical run with `forms: false` gives
  identical numbers, and `BENCHMARK.md` records buffalo going 116 → 74 when P1 landed.
- ⚠ **A12 got slightly worse at P4**: an orphan has `guardianId === null`, so nobody
  weights it up.
- **Two tests were deleted at the user's instruction** and their coverage is gone:
  determinism through the **age-death** path, and the **no-timer** tripwire.
- **Nine determinism guards are byte-for-byte the same test** in nine files. First
  place to cut if the suite needs it.
- ✅ **A61, A56 and the silent group cap are closed.** `A43` is narrowed. `A80`
